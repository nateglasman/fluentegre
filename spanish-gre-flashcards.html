What makes these Spanish GRE flashcards different

Most GRE flashcard apps give you an English word and an English definition. That works fine if you think in English. But if you're a Spanish speaker, you're constantly translating in your head — and that extra step slows you down and weakens retention.

fluenteGRE takes a different approach. Every GRE word comes with a carefully chosen Spanish translation, a definition written entirely in Spanish, and a bilingual memory trick that connects the English word to something familiar in Spanish. You're not just memorizing — you're building bridges between languages.

Example: how a Spanish GRE flashcard works

Gregarious
Gregario
EN Fond of company; sociable and outgoing
ES Sociable, que disfruta de la compania de otros
Memory trick: "Grey" (rebanho/grey) means flock — a gregarious person loves being in the flock, surrounded by others.

This dual-encoding approach — seeing the word in both English and Spanish — creates two memory pathways instead of one. Cognitive science research shows this significantly improves vocabulary retention compared to studying in English alone.

Why Spanish speakers need bilingual GRE flashcards

Spanish speakers preparing for the GRE face a unique challenge. The Verbal section tests vocabulary that even many native English speakers don't know — words like "pusillanimous," "perspicacious," and "equanimity." Studying these in English alone means memorizing abstract definitions disconnected from how you actually think.

With Spanish GRE flashcards, abstract words become concrete. "Equanimity" connects to "ecuanimidad" — a word you may already know. "Perspicacious" links to "perspicaz." These cognates and near-cognates are everywhere in GRE vocabulary because both English and Spanish borrowed heavily from Latin. fluenteGRE leverages these connections systematically across all 1,184 words.
