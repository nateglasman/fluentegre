About fluenteGRE

fluenteGRE is a free, browser-based GRE vocabulary trainer built specifically for bilingual learners. If English isn't your first language, studying GRE vocabulary can feel like memorizing words in a vacuum — disconnected from how you actually think and process language. fluenteGRE fixes that.

The idea is simple: every GRE word comes with a carefully chosen translation in your native language, a definition written in your language, and a mnemonic trick that bridges the two languages. This dual-encoding approach helps you learn faster and remember longer.

Each translation includes not just the word itself, but a full definition in the target language and a language-specific memory trick. For example, a Spanish speaker learning "gregarious" will see the connection to "grey" (rebaño/grey in Spanish means flock), reinforcing the idea of someone who loves being in a group.

Study Modes

Flashcards with Spaced Repetition — flip through cards and rate your confidence (Again, Hard, Good, Easy). Words you struggle with automatically appear more often. Keyboard shortcuts (Space to flip, 1-4 to rate) keep your flow fast.

Definition Quiz — multiple choice questions that test whether you can identify a word's meaning from four options.

Translation Quiz — given a word in English, pick the correct translation in your chosen language. This mode strengthens the bridge between your native language and English.

Pair Matching — match words to their definitions against the clock. Great for rapid recall practice.

Browse Groups — explore all 1,184 words organized by thematic category. Click any word to see its full entry with bilingual definitions and memory tricks.

Progress Tracking — detailed analytics showing your mastery level, struggle areas, rating breakdowns by category, and per-word history. All stored locally in your browser — no account needed.

Why Bilingual GRE Prep Works

Research in cognitive science shows that dual-language encoding creates stronger memory traces than single-language study. When you learn a word in both English and your native language, you're creating two retrieval pathways instead of one. This is especially powerful for abstract GRE vocabulary — words like "equanimity" or "perspicacious" that don't have obvious real-world anchors become much more concrete when connected to a familiar word in your language.

For the millions of non-native English speakers who take the GRE each year, this approach can be the difference between a vocabulary section that feels impossible and one that feels manageable.

Who Built This

fluenteGRE was built by a GRE student who got tired of vocabulary tools that only work in English. It's free, open, and built with the belief that graduate school admissions shouldn't be harder just because English is your second language.
