What makes these Portuguese GRE flashcards different

Most GRE flashcard apps are English-only. If you're a Portuguese speaker, you're forced to memorize abstract English definitions without any anchor to how you actually think. That's slow and frustrating.

fluenteGRE gives every GRE word a Portuguese translation, a definition written in Portuguese, and a memory trick that bridges both languages. You're leveraging your native language to learn faster, not fighting against it.

Example: how a Portuguese GRE flashcard works

Perspicacious
Perspicaz
EN Having a ready insight into and understanding of things; perceptive
PT Que tem facilidade em perceber e compreender as coisas; perceptivo e astuto
Memory trick: "Perspicaz" is nearly identical in Portuguese — if you know perspicaz, you already know perspicacious. The Latin root perspicere (to see through) is shared by both languages.

Portuguese and English share thousands of Latin-derived words. fluenteGRE systematically exploits these cognates across all 1,184 GRE vocabulary words, giving Brazilian and Portuguese speakers a built-in advantage.

Why Portuguese speakers need bilingual GRE flashcards

Brazil sends tens of thousands of students to US graduate programs every year, and the GRE Verbal section is often the biggest barrier. Words like "equanimity," "recalcitrant," and "magnanimous" feel impossible in English — but they connect to "equanimidade," "recalcitrante," and "magnanimo" in Portuguese. These aren't coincidences. English and Portuguese both inherited massive Latin vocabularies, and GRE words are disproportionately Latin-derived.

fluenteGRE turns this into a systematic study method. Instead of memorizing 1,184 disconnected English words, you're activating knowledge you already have in Portuguese and extending it.
